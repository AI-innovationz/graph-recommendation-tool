A hospital is a healthcare facility that provides medical services for the prevention, diagnosis, treatment, and recovery of diseases and injuries. Hospitals serve people of all ages and provide both emergency and routine medical care. A hospital is divided into several departments, including the emergency department, outpatient department, inpatient ward, intensive care unit, operation theatre, laboratory, radiology department, pharmacy, and rehabilitation unit. Each department performs specific medical functions and works together to provide complete patient care.

The emergency department treats patients with serious injuries, accidents, heart attacks, strokes, burns, poisoning, and other life-threatening conditions. The outpatient department examines patients who do not require hospital admission. Patients with serious medical conditions are admitted to inpatient wards or intensive care units for continuous monitoring and treatment. Doctors examine patients, identify symptoms, diagnose diseases, prescribe medicines, and recommend medical procedures. Nurses monitor vital signs, administer medicines, assist during medical procedures, and provide daily patient care. Surgeons perform operations in the operation theatre using sterilized surgical instruments. Anesthesiologists administer anesthesia before surgical procedures and monitor patients during surgery.

The laboratory performs blood tests, urine tests, stool tests, and other diagnostic investigations. The radiology department conducts X-rays, ultrasound scans, CT scans, MRI scans, and mammography to detect diseases and injuries. Pharmacists dispense medicines according to medical prescriptions and provide instructions for proper medication use. Medical equipment such as ventilators, ECG machines, infusion pumps, defibrillators, wheelchairs, and patient monitors supports diagnosis, treatment, and patient care.

Hospitals maintain electronic medical records that include patient history, laboratory reports, imaging reports, prescriptions, allergies, diagnoses, and discharge summaries. Infection control includes hand hygiene, sterilization of medical instruments, waste segregation, and the use of personal protective equipment. After treatment, patients receive discharge instructions, follow-up appointments, rehabilitation services, vaccination recommendations, dietary advice, and preventive healthcare guidance to support long-term recovery and overall well-being.

An emergency medical situation occurs when a person develops a sudden illness or suffers a serious injury that requires immediate medical attention. Common medical emergencies include heart attacks, strokes, severe bleeding, burns, poisoning, allergic reactions, fractures, and road accidents. During an emergency, the first priority is to ensure the safety of the patient and the people nearby.

A bystander should immediately call emergency medical services and provide the patient's location, age, symptoms, and the nature of the emergency. Until professional help arrives, trained individuals may perform first aid or cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR) if necessary. People should avoid moving patients with suspected spinal injuries unless there is immediate danger.

Paramedics assess the patient's condition, monitor vital signs, provide oxygen therapy, control bleeding, administer emergency medications, and transport the patient to the nearest hospital. Inside the emergency department, doctors rapidly examine the patient, diagnose the condition, order laboratory tests and imaging studies, and begin appropriate treatment.

Patients with life-threatening conditions may be admitted to the intensive care unit for continuous monitoring. Surgeons perform emergency operations when internal bleeding, severe trauma, or organ damage is present. Nurses regularly monitor vital signs, administer medicines, and assist with patient care throughout treatment.

Medical equipment such as defibrillators, ventilators, ECG machines, infusion pumps, and patient monitors supports diagnosis and emergency treatment. Blood banks provide compatible blood for patients who require transfusions. Radiology departments conduct X-rays, CT scans, and MRI scans to identify fractures, internal injuries, and other medical conditions.

After the patient's condition becomes stable, doctors provide discharge instructions, prescribe medicines, recommend follow-up appointments, and advise lifestyle modifications to reduce the risk of future medical emergencies.
